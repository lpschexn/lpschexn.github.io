Research

What I Work On

Autonomous vehicles hold enormous potential for reducing transportation energy consumption through optimized driving behavior — but realizing those gains requires sensing systems that remain reliable in exactly the conditions where they are most needed: winter weather, low visibility, and high-speed highway environments. My research addresses this gap directly, building lidar-based perception pipelines that enable safe vehicle following and platooning while treating energy efficiency as a first-class design constraint.

Concretely, I investigate how lidar-derived road grade previews can replace or augment map-based inputs to vehicle energy management systems, and I have evaluated 900 nm versus 1550 nm lidar performance under real arctic winter conditions — the kind of empirical, hardware-grounded work that bridges sensor physics and system-level autonomy. These studies are conducted on public roads and test tracks in the Upper Peninsula of Michigan, where the environment provides natural stress tests unavailable in warmer climates.

The larger goal is a class of autonomous vehicles that are not just capable but measurably more sustainable: systems that preview road geometry to minimize unnecessary acceleration, that platoon safely to cut aerodynamic drag, and that degrade gracefully when sensors are partially obscured. Looking ahead, I am interested in extending these perception and sensor fusion methods into sustainability-focused applications and, eventually, medical and biomedical sensing — where the same rigor applied to detecting road features must be applied to detecting physiological ones.

Looking Ahead

Research Vision

I am actively seeking postdoctoral and faculty positions beginning in 2026–2027, with a focus on autonomous systems, sustainable transportation, and sensing & perception. My near-term goal is to extend the lidar and sensor fusion methods developed during my PhD toward a broader class of energy-critical applications — systems where robust, low-latency sensing directly translates to reduced resource consumption or improved safety outcomes.

In parallel, I am interested in developing a research agenda at the intersection of robust perception and human health. The same pipeline engineering that enables a vehicle to perceive road grade in a snowstorm has structural analogues in medical sensing: detecting subtle signals in noisy environments, fusing heterogeneous modalities, and operating reliably at the edge. I see a genuine opportunity to bridge these domains, and I am looking for environments — national labs, R1/R2 research universities — that support that kind of cross-disciplinary ambition.

I am particularly drawn to institutions and groups where sustainability is not just a framing device but a genuine research priority: reducing emissions through intelligent logistics, enabling energy-efficient mobility in underserved or extreme-climate regions, and building autonomous systems that earn public trust through transparency and measured performance. If your work touches any of these areas, I would be glad to connect.
